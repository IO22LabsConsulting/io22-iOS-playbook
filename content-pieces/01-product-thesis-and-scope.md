# Product Thesis and Scope: The Fastest Path to a Useful iOS App

Most iOS products fail before code quality even matters. The failure is usually scope, not engineering.

A useful product thesis answers three questions:

1. Who is this for?
2. What painful job are they hiring this app to do?
3. What measurable change should happen if the app works?

If your thesis cannot be understood in one paragraph, the product is not ready.

## Scope Like an Operator

Use a three-column feature table:

- Must-have: needed for first successful user outcome
- Nice-to-have: improves experience but not required
- Not now: tempting ideas that are postponed

A practical test: if a feature does not contribute to activation, quality, or retention in the first 30 days, move it out of MVP.

## Define Success Early

Choose metrics before development:

- Activation: first-time completion of core task
- Engagement: weekly recurrence of core behavior
- Quality: crash-free sessions and low task abandonment

You are building for signal, not for volume. Early metrics should explain behavior, not impress with vanity numbers.

## Why This Matters

Teams that narrow scope ship faster, gather cleaner feedback, and adapt more effectively.
Wide-scope teams spend months integrating features they cannot evaluate clearly.

The iOS teams that win are rarely the ones with the largest v1. They are the ones with the sharpest product thesis and the most disciplined scope.
